# Agent Resources - starter playbook

SOPs 1-5 are the four agent lanes. SOPs 6-11 are the human-contractor secondary lane. Every SOP is `(candidate)` until a passed review cites it by name.

## The four lanes

1. Write the hiring case before anyone is asked to ratify a seat. A proposed seat arrives as a written case naming four things: the work that demands it (counted board rows or named deliverables, never a feeling), the facet it fills, the review triangle it preserves or breaks, and a draft role card. The case ends in a recommendation - and "not yet, here are the triggers that would change that" is a good one. Opening the seat is the founder's; **hire-an-agent** and **open-a-department** execute after their word. (candidate)

2. Onboard to the bar before the first task. A newly opened seat completes three research lanes - its domain craft, the competitive reality, and the actual market its department touches - before it is dispatched anything, with a 3-source minimum per lane and a 90-day recency window - anything older than 90 days is still usable, but its age is stated rather than hidden. Depth is verified by spot re-check, not by the seat's own say-so, and the findings land in the card and the playbook rather than in a report nobody reads. (candidate)

3. Maintain the cards on a cadence, not on a complaint. Every seat card carries all six required H2s and a live `## Seat memory` ledger. The maintenance sweep walks all cards against that shape, verifies the ledgers are append-only against backup, and re-proves the review triangle is intact in every department. Drift becomes a filed board row with the file and line named - never a shrug, never a silent fix. (candidate)

4. Read a seat against its own record, and hand back one amendment. An improvement read draws only on evidence the seat produced: deliverables in its outbox, review verdicts in `records/reviews.md`, warnings in `records/warnings.md` attributable to it, and its own `## Seat memory` ledger. It ends in one proposed card or playbook amendment with the evidence cited. Never a complaint, always an amendment. (candidate)

5. Propose; never edit another room's card. A card amendment for another department's seat is drafted here, reviewed by that department's Manager, and - where it changes a seat's official name or scope - ratified by the founder. When the subject is one of this department's own three seats, the review conflict-routes to an independent out-of-department reviewer, the Operations Manager by default. (candidate)

## Secondary lane: human contractors

6. Brief before work. No human starts work for the company without a written brief: the outcome in plain words, the deliverable, numbered acceptance checks, the deadline, and the agreed rate. A contractor who can misread the scope will - the brief exists so they cannot. (candidate)

7. Check the rate before you recommend it. Every rate recommendation carries at least two comparison points, each with a named source - a marketplace listing, a past invoice, a quote the founder received. An unsourced number is labeled `[best guess]` and says so; a blank beats an invented figure. **research-desk** owns the sourcing discipline when the hunt goes outside. (candidate)

8. Review every delivery. When a contractor hands work in, the Manager walks it against the brief's acceptance checks - pass or fail per check - and the review lands as one dated bullet in `memory.md`. Good contractors earn their next brief on the record, not on vibes. (candidate)

9. Keep the roster true. Whenever a human is engaged, reviewed, or released, refresh the contractor roster in `outbox/` the same session. A roster that lags reality is worse than no roster - the founder plans against it. (candidate)

10. Drafts only, always. Every brief, feedback note, offer, and goodbye is written to the outbox for the founder to send - sending anything to a real person is a reserved decision. So are paying, engaging, and releasing: those queue in `company/decision-log.md` with a recommendation attached. (candidate)

11. Write feedback a person can use. A feedback draft names what the work was asked to do, what it did, and the one change that matters most next time - specific, fair, and free of anything the founder would be embarrassed to send under their own name. **write-well** sharpens the sentences when it matters. (candidate)
