# Operator review - the founder's sign-off walk, one artifact at a time

## When to use
The founder wants to clear the tasks that passed Manager review and now wait at the Operator review stage for their own sign-off. Wake phrases: "werkforce, sign-offs", "operator review", "walk my sign-off queue". Boundary: founders-desk owns the seven reserved decision types (external sends, spending, pricing, public claims, hiring and firing, deleting work, ratifying visions and strategy) - this skill owns only the work-product sign-off walk, the founder's review of a completed deliverable that a department has finished and the Reviewer has passed. Boundary: review-desk and the department Reviewer own Manager review - the certification that the work is correct; this skill starts only after that gate is passed and a row sits at Operator review. A sign-off walk that starts ratifying a vision or approving a spend has left its lane and belongs at the desk.

## What it produces
A walked queue and, per decision the founder actually speaks, one `os/signoff.sh` transaction - and, when that decision signs off a design, plan, or eval, the one follow-on build row or deferral line named below. Nothing else is written. For each row presented: the task name, the department, how long it has waited, the Reviewer's Manager-review verdict, and the finished deliverable (the outbox .html) linked one click away so the founder reviews the actual work product, not a path (SOP 11, the founder's link-artifacts rule). A signed-off task moves to Done with a receipt naming what was produced, where it lives, and who reviewed. When the signed-off row is a design, plan, or eval rather than a build, its sign-off also produces one more thing: either a new build row filed on the owning board, or a logged explicit deferral naming why none was filed - never neither (founder doctrine, 2026-07-24; escalated 2026-07-25). A sent-back task reopens to In progress with the operator's verbatim reason noted, its history intact. When present, `os/signoff.sh` is the one write door: the caller supplies the owning board, stable row id, exact row preimage, expected stage, and caller-stable idempotency key; the writer atomically commits the event, board row, applicable human ledgers, machine receipt, and regenerated views or rolls all of them back. On an older HQ with no `os/signoff.sh`, the legacy ledger writes below remain the graceful compatibility path; absence of the new writer never crashes or blocks a founder decision.

## Steps
1. Read the facts fresh from their authorities: every `departments/*/board.md` for rows whose Stage is exactly `Operator review`, and for each such row its brief in `departments/{slug}/briefs.md`, its Reviewer verdict in `departments/{slug}/memory.md`, and the deliverable pair its receipt names in `departments/{slug}/outbox/`. Never walk the queue from memory. For every row, compute the stable id exactly as the dashboard does (department prefix plus the first four hex characters of SHA-1 over `{task text}\0{filed date}`) and the SHA-256 of the complete live board-row line; retain both as the caller's optimistic-concurrency preimage. Never substitute a line number or fuzzy title. If a receipt names a file, confirm the file exists before presenting the row; a receipt pointing at a missing artifact is a warning line to records/warnings.md, not a row the founder can sign off blind. Also check `records/artifact-registry.md` for a line matching this row's outbox path - the artifact mandate (founder law, 2026-07-23) means a PASSed row should already carry one.
2. Order the queue oldest first by the row's Filed date - the founder walks the work that has waited longest before the work that just arrived. State the count up front ("3 tasks await your sign-off") so the founder knows the size of the walk.
3. Present each row one at a time, never as a batch to approve in bulk: task name, department, days waited, the Manager-review verdict quoted, and the deliverable one click away - the registry's claude.ai artifact URL alongside the outbox .html when a registry line exists. If a PASSed row reaches this walk with no registry line yet, I publish it now and add the line before presenting, or, if publishing is unavailable here, I present from the outbox .html with one honest sentence that no artifact link exists for this row. The founder reviews the actual delivered work product on their screen before deciding - a sign-off given without the artifact shown is exactly the failure SOP 14 exists to prevent. When the row is a design, plan, or eval rather than a finished build, I say so plainly and never let the founder infer it - "you are signing off on a design, not a build" - and I close the row's presentation with the next step in the founder's own terms, not doctrine jargon: "If you sign: {the build row that files, or the deferral that gets logged, named specifically} happens next." Wait for the founder's call on this row before moving to the next.
4. On a sign-off, first resolve the REQUIRED 8-hex artifact id from the matching `records/artifact-registry.md` line. The id comes from publishing the finished `.html`; sign-off cannot precede artifact publication. If there is no registry id, publish and register the artifact first or stop with an honest warning - never omit `--artifact`, invent an id, or move the row to Done. If `os/signoff.sh` exists, run `os/signoff.sh --event signoff --dept {department} --row "{row title}" --board departments/{department}/board.md --row-id {stable id} --preimage-sha256 {complete-row SHA-256} --stage-from "Operator review" --stage-to Done --path "{deliverable path(s)}" --artifact {8-hex id} --tree canonical --actor Austin --detail "{one line naming what the operator reviewed}" --idempotency-key {caller-stable key}`. Check that `WROTE ATOMICALLY` covers the board, events, operator reviews, worklog, audit log, machine receipt, and generated views; any nonzero exit means the decision is not yet fully recorded - do not hand-edit around it. If this older HQ has no `os/signoff.sh`, say that the atomic writer is unavailable, then use the legacy compatibility path: read the clock once; append the signed-off line to `records/operator-reviews.md`, the matching receipt line (including date+time, path, artifact id, and tree) to `records/worklog.md`, and one `[review]` line to `records/audit-log.md`; verify all three, then move the board row and apply the same four-field receipt. If the just-signed row was a design, plan, or eval, do not stop there: file the follow-on build row on the owning department's board now (Filed stage, briefed from the signed artifact) or, if the founder's word makes plain no build follows yet, log an explicit deferral line instead (`- YYYY-MM-DD [department] TASK-ID deferred: "{founder's stated reason}"`) - one or the other, every time, never silence. Tell the founder plainly which happened before moving to the next row. Per founder doctrine (2026-07-25 decision-log): a design sign-off IS the implementation go - the filed build row dispatches on its own, with no second sign-off ask, unless it contains an act that is constitutionally reserved (external sends, spend, pricing, public claims, hiring/firing, deleting work, ratifying visions/strategy), which still stops at founders-desk for a runbook before it executes.
5. On a send-back: capture the operator's reason verbatim before anything moves (standing order, founder 2026-07-23; SOP 15). If the founder offers none, ask for it once - the desk never silently accepts a reasonless decline. Use the founder's reason verbatim, or `reason pending - recheck at next morning run` if they cannot give it yet. If `os/signoff.sh` exists, run `os/signoff.sh --event sendback --dept {department} --row "{row title}" --board departments/{department}/board.md --row-id {stable id} --preimage-sha256 {complete-row SHA-256} --stage-from "Operator review" --stage-to "In progress" --path "no-file:operator-sendback" --tree canonical --actor Austin --detail "{reason}" --idempotency-key {caller-stable key}`; sendback does not require `--artifact` because it does not create a Done receipt. Require `WROTE ATOMICALLY`; a nonzero exit leaves the row unmoved. On an older HQ with no writer, append the same sent-back detail to `records/operator-reviews.md` and one `[review]` line to `records/audit-log.md`, verify both, then reopen the row to `In progress` with the equivalent legacy receipt and prior history intact. If a reason-pending line was used, the morning run rechecks until a fresh append carries the real reason.
6. Feed the review-gap system, do not analyze it here. Every sent-back line - and especially its verbatim reason - is the raw material the review-gap analysis (`departments/operations/outbox/*-review-gap-analysis.md`) reads to distill what the Operator bar was looking for that the Manager bar missed. This skill's job is to capture that reason cleanly and completely; the analysis that reads the corpus is a separate deliverable and is not run from this walk. A reason captured as "declined" with no substance starves that system - which is why step 5 refuses it.
7. Close the walk with an honest tally: how many signed off, how many sent back, how many filed reason-pending for the morning run to chase. If the queue was empty, say so plainly - a true empty queue is a real result, not a gap.

## Boundaries
- Never signs off on the operator's behalf. The founder's spoken word is the only thing that appends a signed-off line; the skill presents and records, it does not decide.
- Never batches without showing each artifact. Every row is presented individually with its deliverable one click away; there is no "approve all."
- Never accepts a reasonless decline silently. A send-back without a reason is asked for once, then filed reason-pending and rechecked - never recorded as a bare "declined."
- Never erases history. A send-back reopens the row and annotates it; the ledger is append-only and the board row's prior receipts are not overwritten out of existence.
- founders-desk stays the home of the seven reserved decision types. This skill owns only the work-product sign-off walk and defers any reserved-decision ask to the desk - including a reserved act that surfaces inside a build a design sign-off just greenlit; that act still stops at a runbook even though the rest of the build proceeds without a second ask.
- Never lets a design or plan sign-off go silent on what follows. Every one produces either a filed build row or a logged deferral, and the founder is told which before the walk moves on.

## Passing the standard's four hostile tests
1. **Evidence substitution** - if this skill's on-screen presentation vanished, the outcome is still fully verifiable from durable files: every decision is an append-only line in records/operator-reviews.md, every board row carries its Stage and Receipt, and the audit log points at both. The skill's proof lives in ledgers, not in its own output.
2. **Vague excellence** - each promise is falsifiable. "Oldest first" is checkable against Filed dates; "one click away" is checkable against the linked outbox .html existing; "verbatim reason" is checkable against the sent-back line reading as the founder spoke it; "reopened, not erased" is checkable against the row sitting at In progress with its history present; "design, not a build" is checkable against the presentation actually saying those words before a design/plan sign-off is taken; "auto-filed or deferred" is checkable against a new Filed-stage build row or a deferred line appearing immediately after the ledger's signed-off line, never neither. A reviewer can prove any of these unmet.
3. **Scope creep** - the skill signs off finished work products only. It reopens no settled decision and reaches into no reserved territory: reserved decisions route to founders-desk, Manager review stays with the Reviewer, and the review-gap distillation stays a separate deliverable. It solves the one unstated problem it names and stops.
4. **Self-review** - the skill grades nothing of its own. It records the founder's verdict; it never certifies a deliverable, never overrides a Reviewer, and never marks its own walk correct. The review triangle holds - the operator is the reviewer here, and the skill is the clerk.

## Honest residual risk
The queue read depends on department boards spelling the Stage exactly `Operator review`; a row parked under a drifted stage name is invisible to this walk and would silently never reach the founder - werkforce-checkup's stage-vocabulary check is the backstop, not this skill. The reason-pending path relies on the morning run actually rechecking; if run-the-day is skipped, a deferred reason can sit uncaptured and the review-gap corpus stays thin. And the skill trusts that a receipt's named artifact is the artifact the founder should see - it confirms the file exists but cannot confirm the file is the right or current version, so a stale outbox render could be signed off as if fresh. The skill has not yet run against a live multi-row queue; its oldest-first ordering and one-at-a-time discipline are untested at volume. The design-vs-build call is also a judgment this skill makes from the row's own content (does it name a design/plan/eval, or a shipped build) - a row that is genuinely ambiguous between the two could be mis-sorted, filing a redundant build row for work already built, or skipping the auto-file for work that was in fact only designed; the founder's own read of the presented row is the real backstop.

## What good looks like
A strong walk lets the founder decide each task from the work itself in one motion: the deliverable is on their screen, the Manager verdict is quoted, and every send-back leaves the ledger carrying the reason in the founder's own words. A weak walk lists task names with paths the founder has to hunt down, approves in a batch, or files a decline as "sent back" with no reason - that walk has starved the review-gap system and broken the link-artifacts rule.

## How we know it worked
Every decision the founder spoke used `os/signoff.sh` when present (its `WROTE ATOMICALLY` list complete and its machine receipt present) or the explicitly announced legacy compatibility path when absent; every atomic call carried the unique stable row id, exact preimage, expected stage, owning board, and caller-stable idempotency key; every sign-off carried a real 8-hex artifact id resolved before the call; every sent-back line carries either a verbatim reason or a live reason-pending flag; no row was presented without its deliverable linked; the queue was walked oldest-first; every design/plan/eval sign-off produced exactly one of a filed build row or a logged deferral, and the founder was told which; and this skill wrote nothing outside the writer/legacy surfaces, the one follow-on build row a design/plan sign-off may file, and records/warnings.md - no deliverable authored, no reserved decision touched.
